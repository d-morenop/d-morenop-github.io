About me

I am a Theoretical Physicist working on ice sheet modelling. At the present moment, I work as a Postdoctoral Reseacher at the Laboratoire de Glaciologie of the Univerisité Libre de Bruxelles (ULB). From an ice-sheet modelling perspective, we simulate the future evolution of West Antarctica, particularly Thwaites Glacier. I combine physical descriptions, numerical methods and stochastic processes to understand the stability of Thwaites under a warming climate.

My interests include numerical modelling, grounding line stability, and sea-level projections.

Research

I am interested in the physical processes that dictate the evolution of the Earth System, with a focus on the cryosphere. My work is strongly collaborative, and I am developing the following models:

Nix

A two-dimensional thermomechanical ice sheet model. Nix can be easily forced with any external boundary conditions. It has been benchmarked for standard test problems, showing versatility from regular laptops (lightweight memory allocation) to high-performance computing (multi-threading capabilities).

Publication: The Cryosphere (2024)

Advective-Diffusive Ice Column

Anaytical solutions of the heat equation considering advection, diffusion and strain dissipation.

Publication: Geoscientific Model Development (2025)

Kori-ULB

Higher-order velocity solver: Depth-Integrated Viscosity Approximation (DIVA).

Stochastic climate variability module.

Implementation of a grain-size based tensile stress model.
